# Career & Skills Compass — Research and product basis

Last reviewed: 15 July 2026

## Scope

This research supports the new `/career-skills-compass` experience. The deleted predecessor was not opened or used as a source. The replacement was designed from the stated user purpose, current occupational frameworks, recent labour-market evidence, practitioner writing, and recently published video/podcast material.

The product is intentionally an exploration and skill-planning tool. It is not a clinical or psychometric diagnosis, a hiring guarantee, or a substitute for local licensing, accredited education, supervised practice, immigration rules, or current vacancy research.

## What the evidence changed in the product

1. **Fit is multidimensional.** O*NET separates interests, work values, work styles, abilities, knowledge, skills and work context. The matcher therefore combines life stage, preferred activities, values and environment instead of treating one interest answer as destiny.
2. **Skills should be portable and visible.** OECD and current skills-first hiring evidence support recognising capability beyond degrees and linear titles. Every role therefore includes transferable skills, several entry routes and proof-of-work ideas.
3. **AI changes tasks more reliably than it deletes whole occupations.** The ILO’s task-level work and current McKinsey analysis both point toward large-scale transformation with continued human input. Each career map explains the task shift instead of applying a misleading “AI-proof” label.
4. **Human and technical skills rise together.** WEF, LinkedIn and recent learning research consistently combine AI/data/technology literacy with analytical thinking, adaptability, communication, leadership and collaboration. Each career therefore has four layers: human foundations, transferable capabilities, role craft, and AI/digital fluency.
5. **Exploration should end in action.** Recent practitioner material repeatedly recommends small experiments, portfolio evidence and feedback. Every role includes proof projects and a 30/60/90-day plan.
6. **Stage changes the route, not human potential.** A school student, fresher, experienced professional, returner and career changer need different entry advice even when attracted to the same work. Stage affects guidance and scoring, not which careers are hidden.
7. **Reality checks prevent expensive fantasy.** Job titles are weak proxies for daily experience. Role maps state routine work, pressures, constraints and regulated entry conditions alongside opportunity.

## Product model implemented

### Audiences

- School student
- College, university or vocational learner
- Fresher seeking a first role
- Early-career professional
- Mid-career professional
- Experienced professional or leader
- Person returning after a break
- Career changer
- Freelancer, consultant or business builder

### Preference inputs

- Preferred activities: build/fix, analyse/solve, create/express, help/develop, influence/lead, organise/deliver
- Work values: income, stability, impact, autonomy, mastery, creativity, flexibility, leadership scope
- Work environment: people-rich or independent, structured or ambiguous, desk or active, remote or field
- Life stage, used for route and 90-day-plan adaptation

### Role detail standard

Every career in the library must include:

- what the person actually does;
- entry reality and several plausible routes;
- growth direction;
- an honest reality check;
- how AI changes tasks;
- eight cross-career foundations;
- transferable capabilities;
- role-specific craft;
- AI and digital fluency;
- at least two proof projects or approved demonstrations;
- a stage-aware 30/60/90-day experiment.

### Interpretation boundaries

- A match score represents overlap with the user’s selected preferences, not aptitude, probability of employment or guaranteed satisfaction.
- Regulated careers explicitly require local accredited education, supervision, registration or licensing.
- Salary was excluded because it varies quickly by location, sector, level, organisation and employment model.
- The product favours role families and durable skill maps over volatile vacancy titles.
- Users are encouraged to compare several matches and sample the real work before expensive commitment.

## July 2026 depth pass: additional evidence and implementation

The second build pass followed a requirements audit of the complete interaction, result cards, filters and skill maps. It added two matching dimensions (decision goal and access feasibility), 37 explicit work/task profiles, nine stage-specific playbooks, advanced multi-select filters and skill-level practice/proof guidance. Backend-like hearts, saved counters and local persistence were removed. Comparison is deliberately session-only and labelled “this visit only”.

### July 2026 practical decision tools

- The private vacancy decoder accepts one advert or a two-to-five-ad sample separated by `---`. It reports mapped wording, recurring signals and work conditions without treating a tiny sample as market demand.
- The six-check local reality worksheet records credentials, current hiring evidence, pay ranges, practitioner reality, work authorisation and workplace access or reasonable support. The last check examines actual employer conditions rather than inferring suitability from a job title.
- Every complete role map now includes a private skill-evidence inventory. Users distinguish unassessed, learning, practising and evidence-ready capabilities; the tool summarises all four skill layers, identifies the next three gaps and exports a bounded plan without treating self-report as assessment or role readiness.
- Matcher results can move into a three-career comparison and evidence-backed decision journal in one action. Users can still pin any other combination manually.
- The reverse-skill bridge starts from capabilities a user can evidence and shows exact role-map overlap without changing the preference match score.
- Every role includes a role-to-role transition bridge. It separates named, role-craft and digital overlap; identifies first gaps; checks access-route differences; and produces a translate/build/test plan.
- Every transition result states that wording overlap is not competence, qualification, hiring probability or local demand.

### Revised product standard

Every role, including the new distinct Social Worker & Case Coordinator map, now includes:

- four ordinary task categories, industries and adjacent roles;
- fit signals and multiple explicit friction points;
- portfolio, degree, regulated, vocational or experience-led access routes;
- learning commitment and time-to-credible-entry horizon;
- four complete skill layers, with why, practice and proof for each mapped capability;
- proof projects and a stage-aware 30/60/90-day experiment;
- a task-level AI shift statement without “future-proof” claims.

The matcher now considers stage, decision goal, activity interests, work values, environment, access route and time horizon. Scores are centrality-weighted: a preference that is core to a role counts more than one that is merely compatible. Scores remain sorting aids, never aptitude or employment probabilities.
