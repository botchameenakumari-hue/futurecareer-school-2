# Original visual-system brief (as given by the site owner)

Saved here so the instructions travel with the audit. The page reports in this folder are measured against this brief. Where a page report says "required action", it is the minimum needed to satisfy this brief.

Create and wire a complete visual system for the following pages and route patterns:
[PASTE LINKS, URLS, OR ROUTE PATTERNS HERE]

Act as a world-class editorial designer, art director, UX designer, SEO specialist, and front-end implementation expert.

Build a premium, editorial, natural, human-designed visual system for Future Career School. The brand helps students, parents, graduates, and working professionals make practical career decisions, build high-value and transferable skills, create proof of ability through real work, and reach earlier financial freedom through a compounding skill portfolio.

The visual system must communicate:

* Career decisions based on evidence rather than guesswork.
* High-value and transferable skills.
* Building proof through projects and real work.
* Skill portfolios that compound over time.
* Earlier financial freedom through practical career choices.
* Career fit, market demand, cost, runway, learning curve, opportunity, and trade-offs.
* Mentoring, counselling, reflection, and informed decision-making.
* Human judgment, adaptability, and meaningful work.
* Realistic Indian student, family, graduate, and professional contexts.

Do not give generic advice such as "choose a course," "join a college," or "follow your passion" without showing the actual work, skills, market relevance, cost, trade-offs, and evidence required to make a sound decision.

## Blog visual requirements

For every eligible blog article, create and wire:

* One natural editorial hero image.
* Five to seven supporting visuals as the normal target.
* Up to ten supporting visuals when the article genuinely needs more explanation.

The hero should:

* Reflect the article's actual audience and situation.
* Work for search previews, social previews, and the page header.
* Be visually attractive and clickable.
* Prefer a natural human or real-world scene.
* Avoid long text.
* Use a short, accurate title treatment only when it genuinely improves the design.

Keep existing natural, editorial hero images when they are good. Do not replace good hero images unnecessarily. Add supporting visuals without replacing strong existing imagery. Replace an existing image only when it is clearly generic, misleading, repetitive, broken, or visually inconsistent.

The non-hero visuals must primarily help readers understand the article quickly without reading every paragraph. They must not be decorative assets. Derive their content from the article and use the format that best matches the actual idea, including: infographics, graphs and charts, comparison tables, Venn diagrams, flowcharts, linear process chains, chronological timelines, pyramids and funnels, matrix grids, callout quote boxes, asymmetrical pros-and-cons layouts, self-assessment checklists, iconic arrays, decision trees, roadmaps, step-by-step explainers, skill maps, career progression visuals, evidence-to-outcome diagrams, before-and-after comparisons, framework cards, short text-led explanatory panels.

Use these visuals to explain real article ideas, such as: comparing two career options; showing a career decision process; explaining skill progression; showing how skills become projects, proof, opportunities, and income; comparing cost, time, risk, learning value, and earning potential; explaining timelines or stages; showing relationships between interests, skills, roles, and career outcomes; summarising the article's key takeaways; helping readers assess their own situation; showing the difference between a weak and strong career decision; explaining a roadmap after 10th, after 12th, after graduation, or during a career change.

Use natural human/editorial scenes as additional supporting imagery where they establish emotion, context, trust, or realism. They must complement explanatory graphics, not replace them.

The correct hierarchy is:

1. Hero image: natural, premium, human, editorial, emotionally engaging, and suitable for Google and social previews.
2. Informational non-hero visuals: diagrams, infographics, charts, tables, frameworks, timelines, checklists, comparisons, and other explanatory formats based on the article.
3. Optional contextual human scenes: used when they help readers relate to students, parents, graduates, counsellors, or working professionals.

For every non-hero visual:

* Derive the content from the article.
* Use short, accurate, readable text.
* Use correct spelling and grammar.
* Make the hierarchy clear on desktop and mobile.
* Use realistic numbers only when supported by the article or a reliable source.
* Never invent statistics, salary figures, rankings, or claims.
* Repeat essential information in nearby HTML text or captions.
* Use accessible contrast and readable typography.
* Prevent tables, diagrams, labels, and text from overflowing on mobile.
* Use optimized WebP or another suitable web format.
* Wire every asset to the correct blog route.

## Art direction (most of it might not be applicable for non-hero images)

Use believable Indian people, environments, gestures, clothing, objects, work surfaces, and circumstances. Prefer: natural daylight and believable indoor light; documentary and editorial photography; real home study spaces; counselling rooms; neighbourhood workspaces; classrooms; offices; libraries; cafés; family dining tables; modest professional environments; paper notes, portfolios, job descriptions, course information, budgets, notebooks, project work, tools, laptops, feedback, books, and real work artifacts; imperfect human gestures and candid expressions; varied ages, genders, locations, crops, lighting, compositions, and environments; restrained palettes such as ink blue, forest green, muted olive, clay, saffron, cream, charcoal, walnut, faded blue, and warm neutrals; visual hierarchy and compositions that work with surrounding page copy.

Avoid: repetitive centred portraits; identical desks and identical laptop scenes; generic handshakes; fake smiles and corporate stock poses; neon gradients; glowing holograms; floating icons; robots; sci-fi interfaces; glossy 3D objects; fake dashboards; futuristic clichés; excessively smooth faces or plastic skin; perfectly symmetrical flat lays; decorative visuals that do not teach anything; overloaded images with too many objects; inaccurate or invented statistics; text that is too small to read; keyword-stuffed image copy; identical AI-generated-looking layouts across every article; repetitive AI aesthetics, AI gradients, meaningless icons, excessive decoration, or generic stock-photo treatments.

## Text-bearing raster visuals

Create raster images containing short contextual text whenever they improve comprehension (at-a-glance summaries, "what this means" cards, brief decision principles, short skill or evidence summaries, compact article-specific explanations, carefully designed contextual cards with accurate labels). They must: use short, useful, readable text; be factually consistent with the article; use correct spelling and grammar; have clear typography and sufficient contrast; repeat essential information in nearby HTML text or a caption; never hide essential information only inside an image; avoid excessive text and tiny labels; never use keyword stuffing.

Use diagrams only where they explain a real relationship that is difficult to understand through prose or photography. Preserve useful existing diagrams instead of deleting them.

## Repository and implementation requirements

* Inspect the existing repository structure before making changes.
* Preserve the existing site, layout, navigation, typography, CTAs, components, good imagery, and working diagrams unless a change is genuinely necessary.
* Do not create unused image files.
* Wire every final asset into the actual page or shared component that uses it.
* Do not replace good existing hero imagery merely because supporting visuals are being added.
* Add additional imagery wherever it improves comprehension and the overall experience.
* Do not mess up existing page code. If a non-image code change appears genuinely necessary, identify the mistake and explain it before changing unrelated code.
* Preserve the existing framework and build system.

Every image must have: a meaningful filename; descriptive, human-written SEO alt text; a useful human-written title attribute; a concise caption whenever the image teaches or explains something; correct route-specific meaning; optimized WebP or another web-friendly format; sensible dimensions and compression; explicit width and height attributes where appropriate; lazy loading below the fold; eager loading or fetch priority only for important hero images; no broken or missing references.

Alt text must describe what is visibly present and why it is relevant. Do not use vague alt text such as "career image" or "student image."

## Responsive and technical requirements

Preserve mobile, tablet, desktop, and wide-screen compatibility. Make images scale responsively without distortion (width: 100% with appropriate height behaviour). Ensure captions wrap correctly, text-bearing cards do not overflow, diagrams remain readable on mobile, no fixed-width layouts that break on narrow screens. Check for layout shifts, broken links, clipped images, and horizontal overflow. Do not spoil navigation, CTAs, typography, or content flow. Use the existing framework's responsive conventions. Verify representative mobile and desktop layouts.
